BEFORE YOU SIGN

Korea rental contract checklist for foreign tenants

A Seoul rental can look straightforward—price, deposit, move-in date—but the important risks are often in the details. Use this checklist before transferring a large deposit or signing a contract. It is designed for foreign renters who may not know which Korean documents and questions matter.

Checklist1. Identify the property2. Verify the owner3. Check the money4. Read the contract5. Prepare move-in steps

1. Confirm the exact property and legal address

Do not rely only on a listing title such as “Gangnam one-room” or a building nickname. Write down the exact address and unit number that will appear in the contract. Make sure the unit you viewed is the unit you are renting. In mixed-use buildings, also confirm the property category and whether the use of the unit matches what the broker described.

Inspect the physical condition before signing. Test water pressure, hot water, heating or cooling controls, windows, locks, appliances, and visible signs of moisture or mold. Photograph existing damage and send a dated record to the landlord or agent so it is less likely to become a dispute at move-out. Ask which repairs will be completed before you receive the keys and put promises that matter into writing.

2. Verify the owner and property rights

The name of the person receiving your deposit should make sense in relation to the registered owner and the contract. If somebody else is signing or receiving money on behalf of the owner, ask for documentation showing their authority. For meaningful deposits, review the property registry and understand whether mortgages, seizures, or other registered rights could affect the property. If you cannot interpret the registry confidently, get independent help.

This check is especially important for jeonse or high-deposit wolse. The existence of a property does not mean every deposit is equally protected. The relationship among property value, secured debt, senior claims, and your deposit can matter. KoreaHomeGuide does not assess legal priority, so use an official service or qualified professional when the deposit is material to your finances.

3. Separate deposit, rent, maintenance fees, and brokerage

Write every recurring and one-time payment separately. Confirm the deposit, monthly rent, due date, management fee, utilities included in that fee, parking charges, internet, cleaning, key or card replacement charges, and any other recurring item. A low advertised rent can be less attractive after a large management fee is added.

Brokerage is a separate cost. Seoul publishes ceilings for rental brokerage commissions. The actual commission can be negotiated at or below the applicable ceiling. Ask the broker to explain the transaction value, percentage, and whether VAT is separate before you pay. KoreaHomeGuide’s calculator can estimate the ceiling from your deposit and monthly rent, but the signed brokerage agreement and current official schedule control the actual transaction.

4. Read the full contract, including special clauses

Check the names, identification details, exact property, deposit, rent, payment dates, lease start and end, and handover date. Then read the special clauses. Important issues include early termination, renewal, responsibility for repairs, restoration at move-out, pets, subletting, smoking, parking, furniture, appliances, and what happens if move-in is delayed.

If the agent says “that is standard” but the clause matters to you, ask what it means in plain language. A verbal promise is harder to rely on than a written clause. If you need an English or Chinese explanation, treat the translated explanation as a convenience and make sure you understand which Korean document is legally being signed. For a large deposit or unusual clause, independent legal or professional review can be worth the cost.

Before transferring the balance: re-check the property and owner information if there has been a meaningful gap since the first review. Do not assume nothing changed between viewing, contract signing, and final payment.

5. Prepare move-in, residence reporting, and deposit-protection steps

Ask what administrative steps apply after you move in. Korean residents often hear terms such as move-in reporting, fixed date, or residence registration in discussions of rental protection. Foreign residents can have different procedures depending on immigration status and the document involved. Confirm the current process with the district office, immigration authority, or a qualified adviser rather than copying a checklist written for a Korean citizen.

If you plan to use a deposit guarantee or protection product, check eligibility before assuming it will cover the contract. Some products have property-value, debt, timing, or documentation conditions. Keep copies of the signed contract, payment evidence, broker details, property information, and communication with the landlord.

FAQ

Is an English contract enough?

Ask which version is the operative signed contract and make sure the Korean terms you are agreeing to match your understanding. For important disputes, professional review is safer than relying on an informal translation.

Should I send a deposit before checking the registry?

For a meaningful deposit, verify the property and party receiving the money before transferring funds. The exact legal checks depend on the transaction.

Can KoreaHomeGuide verify a landlord?

No. KoreaHomeGuide provides price references and educational checklists, not identity verification or legal due diligence.
