What is an officetel?

An officetel is a Korean mixed-use building category designed for office or residential use depending on the unit and registration. Many residential units function like compact studios. Do not assume that every building marketed as an officetel has identical legal use, facilities or tenant protections. Confirm the specific unit and its registered use.

Deposit and monthly rent move together

A typical wolse quote combines a deposit with monthly rent. A landlord may accept a higher deposit for a lower monthly payment, or the reverse. That means two listings with the same rent are not directly comparable if their deposits differ. Compare the full structure, not just the largest number on the listing.

Management fees can change the answer

Officetel management fees may cover common-area cleaning, security, elevators, internet or some utilities, but the included items vary. Heating, cooling, electricity, water and parking can be billed separately. Ask for recent itemized bills for the exact unit or a comparable occupied unit. A fixed “management fee” without a clear breakdown deserves another question.

Korea's standard housing lease form was revised so a fixed monthly management fee of KRW 100,000 or more can be broken down into common management, utilities and other charges. Treat that as a documentation aid, not proof that the advertised number is complete. Ask the broker or landlord to carry the breakdown into the written explanation and contract, identify usage-based items separately, and keep a recent bill or photo of the building's fee notice.

Calculate the real monthly cost

Use one total for every quote: monthly rent + fixed management fee + average separately billed utilities + internet + parking. Keep one-time move-in, cleaning and furniture costs in a separate cash-needed total so they do not disappear from the decision.

For example, a KRW 900,000 rent with a KRW 150,000 management fee, KRW 80,000 average utilities and KRW 30,000 internet costs about KRW 1,160,000 per month before parking. A KRW 950,000 unit with lower fees can therefore be cheaper overall. These figures are an illustration, not a Seoul average; use recent bills from the exact unit and check both summer and winter.

What should I ask about the management fee?

Ask for the fixed amount, every included item, separately metered utilities, recent bills, seasonal variation, internet, parking and any move-in or move-out charge. Even when an advertisement shows a management-fee number, treat it as the start of the breakdown rather than the final monthly cost.

Usable area may feel smaller

Listings can display several area figures. Exclusive floor area is more useful for understanding the space you actually occupy than a large supply-area headline. Visit in person and consider storage, pillars, hallways, window placement and furniture. A 25㎡ unit with a poor layout can feel smaller than a well-planned unit with a similar number.

Check noise, ventilation and building rules

Compact high-rise living makes ventilation, cooking odors, elevator noise and neighboring commercial activity important. Visit at the time you expect to be home. Check windows, water pressure, drainage, air-conditioning, heating method, waste disposal, delivery access and move-in rules. Ask whether short-term rentals or heavy office use operate nearby.

Budget for signing and moving

In addition to the deposit and first rent, plan for brokerage fees, moving, cleaning, utilities, internet, parking and replacement of missing furniture. Use the brokerage fee calculator to estimate the applicable ceiling. Residential officetels meeting specified size and facility conditions have a separate brokerage-rate category; other cases may be treated differently, so describe the exact unit to the broker and request the calculation in writing.

Before you transfer the deposit

Verify the owner, registry, exact unit, receiving account, contract terms and applicable deposit-protection procedures. Write included appliances, repair promises and management-fee items into the agreement. Follow the before-you-sign guide rather than assuming a modern building is automatically low-risk.

FAQ

Is every officetel registered and used the same way?

No. Confirm the exact unit's registered use, facilities and contract terms rather than relying only on the listing label.

Are utilities included in an officetel management fee?

Not necessarily. Ask for the itemized fee and recent bills because electricity, heating, cooling, water, parking and internet treatment varies.

Should I compare an officetel quote with apartment rents?

Use officetel contracts in the same district and a similar size and deposit range first. Apartments and low-rise homes are different rental markets.

Best comparison

Use the same district, officetel category, similar exclusive area, similar deposit and recent contract months. Then compare the all-in monthly cost.
