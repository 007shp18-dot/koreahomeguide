How to rent an apartment in Korea as a foreigner

The sequence is budget, housing type, quote check, property/owner verification, contract, payment, and move-in procedures. Foreigners can rent homes in Korea, but important checks happen before money changes hands.

1. Set a total housing budget

Do not compare homes by monthly rent alone. Write down the deposit you can lock up, monthly rent, management fee, utilities, brokerage fee, moving cost and any furniture you must buy. A cheaper headline rent can be more expensive after a high management fee. Keep emergency cash separate from the deposit.

2. Choose the rental structure

Wolse normally combines a deposit with monthly rent. Jeonse uses a much larger deposit with little or no monthly rent. Some contracts sit between the two. The correct choice depends on your available cash, visa horizon, risk tolerance and financing. Read the wolse versus jeonse guide before treating a large deposit as a bargain.

3. Understand housing labels

An apartment usually means a unit in a standardized apartment complex. An officetel is a compact mixed-use building popular with single residents. Villa often refers to a low-rise multi-unit home, not a luxury villa. One-room describes the layout, not always the official building category. Confirm the registered property type rather than relying only on the listing label.

4. Prepare documents and questions

Agents may ask for a passport, Korean phone number, local contact details and, when available, a Residence Card. Requirements vary by landlord and contract. Ask whether foreign registration, address reporting, pets, parking, subletting or early termination affect the deal. If you cannot read Korean, arrange an independent translator before contract day.

5. Compare the quote with signed contracts

Listing prices are asking prices. Compare the same district, housing type, floor area, deposit and recent contract period. Use the Seoul Rent Check as a market reference, then ask why a unit differs because of floor, furnishings, condition, station access or management fees.

6. Verify before paying

Confirm the exact address, owner identity, property registry, existing secured debt, unpaid taxes where relevant, deposit-protection options and the bank account receiving money. Do not let urgency replace verification. A small reservation payment can still be difficult to recover. Follow the before-you-sign checklist and obtain professional help when the deposit is material to you.

7. Read every contract term

Check the deposit, rent, payment date, management-fee items, contract period, repair responsibility, move-in condition, special clauses and return of the deposit. Verbal promises should be written into the agreement. Never sign a document you do not understand.

8. Complete the move-in protections

After signing and taking possession, complete the applicable residence or address reporting and fixed-date procedures promptly. Keep the signed contract, transfer records, registry copies, photos and messages together. Exact eligibility and procedure depend on your immigration and housing situation, so confirm current requirements with the relevant public office or a qualified adviser.

FAQ

Can a foreigner sign a residential lease in Korea?

Yes, but landlord, document and payment requirements vary. Verify the exact unit, party receiving money and contract terms before transferring a deposit.

Do I need a Residence Card before I rent?

Some agents or landlords may request one, while registration and move-in timing can differ. Confirm the current administrative steps for your status before contract day.

Does KoreaHomeGuide find apartments or contact landlords?

No. KoreaHomeGuide does not list homes or broker leases. It helps you compare a real quote with official signed transactions and prepare better questions.
