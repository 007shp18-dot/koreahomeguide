RENTING IN YONGSAN-GU

What renters should know about apartments in Yongsan-gu

The district is useful for people who need flexible access to central Seoul, the south side of the Han River, or major railway connections at Yongsan Station. Depending on the neighborhood, subway convenience can be excellent or surprisingly uneven, so station walking time matters.

Price dispersion is unusually wide. International demand, luxury housing, older villas, compact officetels, and redevelopment-era stock can all appear within the same district. A district median is useful as a starting point, but property type, floor area, and exact sub-neighborhood should always be checked.

Apartments are typically the most standardized residential option, with clearer building management, security, elevators, and shared facilities than many smaller buildings. They often suit renters who value predictable building quality, family-sized layouts, parking, and resale-style residential neighborhoods. The downside is that deposits can be larger and small-unit supply may be limited in some districts.

Do not assume a familiar expat neighborhood means a contract is automatically renter-friendly. Verify the registered owner, exact address, deposit protection options, maintenance fees, and whether furniture or utilities that appear in a listing are actually written into the contract. When comparing apartment contracts, pay attention to exclusive-use area, building age, floor, renovation condition, station distance, and whether the contract is monthly rent or jeonse. A nominally similar apartment can still differ materially because of view, floor, or interior condition.
