RENTING IN YONGSAN-GU

What renters should know about villas and multi-family homes in Yongsan-gu

The district is useful for people who need flexible access to central Seoul, the south side of the Han River, or major railway connections at Yongsan Station. Depending on the neighborhood, subway convenience can be excellent or surprisingly uneven, so station walking time matters.

Price dispersion is unusually wide. International demand, luxury housing, older villas, compact officetels, and redevelopment-era stock can all appear within the same district. A district median is useful as a starting point, but property type, floor area, and exact sub-neighborhood should always be checked.

Villa and multi-family housing can offer more space or a quieter residential setting for the same headline rent, but the buildings are much less standardized than large apartment complexes. This category can work well for renters who prioritize floor area, residential streets, or lower monthly rent over concierge-style management. It also includes much of the housing that foreigners informally call one-room or low-rise housing.

Do not assume a familiar expat neighborhood means a contract is automatically renter-friendly. Verify the registered owner, exact address, deposit protection options, maintenance fees, and whether furniture or utilities that appear in a listing are actually written into the contract. Inspect the building in person, confirm the exact registered address and owner, check for moisture or mold, review security and parking, and understand deposit-protection options. Condition can vary substantially even between nearby buildings.
