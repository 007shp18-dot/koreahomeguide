RENTING IN GANGNAM-GU

What renters should know about low-rise homes in Gangnam-gu

For renters working around Gangnam Station, Teheran-ro, COEX, Samsung, or southern Seoul, the district can reduce commute time substantially. Line 2, the Shinbundang Line, Line 9, and multiple bus corridors make it practical for office workers who value direct access more than maximum floor area.

The trade-off is price. A higher monthly rent can buy newer buildings, shorter walks to major stations, better building management, and more predictable amenities. Moving only a few stations away can change the rent-to-space equation quickly, so comparing the exact property type and size is more useful than relying on a single “Gangnam average.”

This page covers Korea's rowhouse and multi-household low-rise category (연립·다세대). These buildings can offer more space or a quieter residential setting, but they are less standardized than large apartment complexes. Detached and multi-family houses (단독·다가구), which include many one-room-style rentals, are a separate official transaction category and are not mixed into these figures.

Check maintenance fees carefully, especially in newer officetels and mixed-use towers. Furnished units, parking, floor level, and building age can create large differences even when two contracts have similar deposits and floor area. Inspect the building in person, confirm the exact registered address and owner, check for moisture or mold, review security and parking, and understand deposit-protection options. Condition can vary substantially even between nearby buildings.
