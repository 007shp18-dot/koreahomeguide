# Dubai content: editorial and evidence memo

Checked 9 September 2026. Read-only research; no repository or database edits. Scope: existing EN/KO rental-yield column, all six Dubai journey articles, Dubai purchase-guide component. No interviews, property visits, or unit-level rent/service-charge verification were performed. No source prose is quoted below.

## Editorial diagnosis

The existing rental-yield article has **1,557 English words and ten H2 sections**. Its main arithmetic is useful and its fee allocation is carefully stated. The weakness is that the article repeatedly proves that a hypothetical case is hypothetical, then adds a second lesson about mortgages and a third about mortgage break-even rent. It is a good calculation worksheet stretched into a column, with no reported evidence explaining why the question matters now.

The six journey articles are approximately 330–396 English words each. They are not excessively long individually; the problem is repetition and lack of decision-specific evidence. Identity checks, matching properties, documented costs, timing and uncertainty are repeated across almost every stage. Some have five or six headings with only one paragraph each, making them feel like generic checklist fragments. The discovery and area-selection articles repeat Marina/JLT/Downtown lifestyle references without offering transport facts, a concrete comparison, or an evidenced trade-off.

Priority: rewrite the rental-yield column around **the rent an owner can actually collect today**, retaining one compact calculation; make the purchase guide a document-to-decision table; give each journey stage one distinct job. Do not simply shorten every piece by the same percentage.

## Four useful empirical observations, verified in original sources

### CBRE original report: three observations (publication 28 July 2026; Q2 2026 observations)

[Report landing page](https://www.cbre.ae/insights/figures/uae-real-estate-market-review-q2-2026)

[Original PDF, printed pages 6–7](https://mktgdocs.cbre.com/2299/d185c834-16cb-4ddd-b411-09c708ea7286-2032937689/CBRE_-_UAE_Real_Estate_Market_.pdf)

1. **Dubai apartment rents fell 6.5% quarter on quarter and 2.4% year on year in Q2 2026.** Villa rents fell 4.0% quarterly and 3.4% annually. Use the apartment series for an apartment column, not the all-residential figure.
2. **Dubai Marina apartment rents fell approximately 10% quarter on quarter; Downtown approximately 11%.** These are community-level movements, not promised reductions for particular leases.
3. **About 18,000 homes were completed across Dubai in H1 2026.** This is delivered supply, not the announced pipeline; it is context for competing rentals, not a local vacancy estimate.

The residential pages attribute data to CBRE Research, REIDIN and Macrobond. The PDF does not expose sample counts, weighting or a reproducible matched-unit rent methodology. Attribute the figures to CBRE, preserve dates and segments, and do not describe them as SignedPrice calculations or multiply them into a particular property's forecast. The search/PDF title metadata incorrectly says Q4 2024, while the cover and internal pages say Q2 2026, July 2026. Use the internal report date and landing page.

### DLD original release: fourth observation

[Dubai rental market, Q1 2026](https://dubailand.gov.ae/en/news-media/dubai-s-rental-market-charts-stable-trajectory-reflecting-integrated-regulatory-environment-and-sustained-public-confidence/), **published 19 April 2026** (not the August date in the search result).

DLD reports **118,385 new rental contracts and 135,607 renewals** in Q1 2026, alongside AED32.2 billion of contract value. These are Dubai-wide administrative contract counts; the release does not provide a residential-only breakdown or unique-home denominator. They support separating new and renewal evidence. They do not establish the probability that a tenant renews, an occupancy rate, or the average collectible rent of an apartment.

**Recommended selection for the column:** one apartment-rent observation plus the new/renewal distinction; use the local area movement or completion count only if it materially advances the story. Four observations are available, but all four need not be inserted.

### Independent context, not additional numerical evidence

[JLL UAE living market dynamics, Q2 2026](https://www.jll.com/en-ae/insights/market-dynamics/uae-living), published **31 July 2026**. Its accessible original summary describes simultaneous quarterly moderation in residential sales prices and rents and increased supply. It supports the direction of the current-market framing; the public page does not expose enough numerical methodology to manufacture an additional figure.

## Proposed rental-yield rewrite

Working title: **두바이 임대수익률 7%, 지금도 그 임대료를 받을 수 있을까?** / **Dubai's 7% rental yield starts with a rent you can collect**.

Target roughly 850–1,050 English words, five substantive sections, one operating-income table, one compact sensitivity table. Preserve the existing canonical URL and publication history.

1. **The rent deserves a fresh check.** Open with the documented apartment-market change, then the reader's decision: a seller's annual rent may describe a current lease, a recently renewed lease, or a hope for the next tenant. State the thesis early: credible income requires the right lease evidence and the building's recurring bill.
2. **Identify which rent is in the brochure.** Three-row table: existing lease → contract dates and receipts; renewal → actual renewal terms; vacant unit → current comparable signed leases/letting evidence. Avoid pretending to have obtained unit files. Explain why the new/renewal distinction matters, using the DLD count only as background.
3. **Follow one year's money.** Keep AED1m price/AED70k annual rent scenario. One compact waterfall/table to AED45,958 operating income; distinguish 4.60% on price from 4.32% on the assumed AED1.065m total cash. Label assumptions once adjacent to table.
4. **The recurring bill can overturn the ranking.** Explain RERA budget + actual statement + arrears and the need to avoid double-counting shared insurance/reserve charges. Keep the existing 3×3 vacancy/service-charge table OR a simpler two-case comparison, not both the prior vacancy bullet list and full table. One sentence explains the AED6,000 bill difference.
5. **What would make this purchase work?** End with four documents and one explicit decision rule: recalculate with supportable rent, actual recurring bill, itemized acquisition costs and finance; compare that result with the return/cash reserve the buyer requires. Link the specialist document guide and financing tool. An off-plan investor needs a separate delivery/letting timeline.

Detailed cuts/moves:

- Merge current opening's two paragraphs into a short thesis + scenario caption; preserve the essential hypothetical label.
- Convert the seven-line money list into a clean table. Remove the prose about a cent's rounding difference by displaying whole dirhams.
- Delete **“Stress-test vacancy before comparing properties”** as a separate section; the following matrix already contains all three cases.
- Merge **“Turn the example into a property decision”** and **“Continue with the Dubai evidence”** into the final action section.
- Move **“A positive operating yield can still leave a cash shortfall”** and **“How much rent would cover the modeled loan payments?”** to a financing guide. If no companion exists yet, retain one short mortgage result or a clearly labelled calculation detail, rather than silently breaking a useful internal link.
- Consolidate repeated exclusions and hypothetical reminders into one short methods note: observed market context versus modelled property case; sources/check date; before-finance income; excluded investor-specific tax, exit costs and capital changes. Keep scenario-specific exclusions next to the relevant number where necessary.
- Replace the long Korean written-out amortization formula and repeated “가정한/모형상/근거” phrasing with the actual question and result. Translate natural sentences rather than mechanically mirroring English hedges.

## Companion guide: documents that change a Dubai rental decision

Working title: **두바이 임대용 집을 사기 전 받아야 할 서류 5가지**.

| Document | Read these fields | Decision it changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current tenancy/Ejari record and payment evidence | Unit, commencement/expiry, annual amount, payment dates, actual receipts | Current collectible rent and timing; not proof of next tenant's rent |
| Approved service-charge budget and unit statement | Project/use/year, billed area, covered components, amount due, arrears | Owner's recurring budget and completion settlement |
| Title and e-NOC for the completed-property route | Registered unit/owner, outstanding requirements, transaction route | Whether offer, seller and registration file match |
| Management/letting proposal | Basis of percentage, minimum fee, renewal/letting charges, scope and VAT | Whether 5% really describes the owner's management cost |
| Itemized transfer/finance statement | Registration allocation, trustee/document costs, brokerage, loan charges, due dates | Total cash needed and liquidity after completion |

No invented completed example from a named tower. A genuinely sourced tower case is a later enhancement once actual signed-rent and approved-charge documents can be accessed.
