# Editorial and collection-operations revision — 14 September 2026

## Scope

- 100 named-property analyses, 25 each in Seoul, Singapore, Dubai and Tokyo: English and Korean headlines and complete essays.
- 25 portfolio analyses, market briefs, monthly reports and September stories: both English and Korean bodies.
- 10 unique database-backed financial and neighbourhood articles: revised English and Korean, including eight previously English-only stories. Existing Chinese editions remain unchanged.
- Eight neighbourhood photo stories, 30 standalone city articles and four city introduction hubs: both languages.
- Three legacy English evidence explainers, whose public legacy routes continue to redirect as before.
- Source presentation in shared article renderers and the collection-operations workspace.

Policy trackers, statutory reference guides, external publishers' headlines, underlying transaction releases and Chinese prose are not reclassified as newly written reporting. Original source files remain available as the evidence/asset baseline; published accessors apply the revision. A newer approved database edit supersedes this dated overlay.

## Reading that informed the edit

The requested [Prowly directory](https://prowly.com/profiles/journalist-lists/top-100-real-estate-journalists-in-the-us) was used to identify writers. Three actual articles were read, including their endings:

- Joshua Fechter, [Texas housing legislation](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/06/16/texas-legislature-housing-bills/): connect a concrete change to who experiences it, while separating enactment from likely effects.
- Kim Velsey, [larger buildings with fewer apartments](https://www.curbed.com/2022/07/bigger-building-fewer-apartments-nyc.html): use a specific contradiction to organise the explanation, rather than a generic feature inventory.
- Katherine McLaughlin, [people who choose to keep renting](https://www.architecturaldigest.com/story/lifetime-renters-share-why-they-choose-not-to-buy-a-home): organise the housing decision around reasons and tradeoffs, not an assumed universal preference for ownership.

These are general editorial techniques. No individual voice was copied, no quotations were imported, and this was not model training or an exhaustive study of all 100 people in the directory.

## Editorial decisions

The opening should establish a question with a consequence. A unit count, approval year or parking ratio is background unless it changes a supported comparison. Name the denominator, acquisition assumptions and time period near any conclusion that depends on them. Do not infer an individual home's appreciation from a changing sales mix.

Banpo Riche previously opened with its approval year, household count and a vague request to check services and routes. The new analysis starts with a comparison against a similar Banpo Xi home, then asks whether a lower price survives repair costs and different daily routes. The 2018 breakfast report remains historical service evidence; it does not establish current availability or a price premium. The article does not invent a matched price comparison that has not been assembled.

Travel and neighbourhood writing distinguishes an attraction from a residential condition. No visit, interview, observation of current noise, current service or current opening time was fabricated. Archival photographs retain dates and credits; a contextual skyline is not labelled as a verified property photograph.

Korean paragraphs were written as Korean prose rather than line-by-line copies of the English syntax. Short briefs can use fewer substantive sections; no filler heading is required to meet a four-section template. Financial examples retain reproducible arithmetic and label assumptions, financing, opportunity cost and missing exit values separately.

## Publication and operations safeguards

References and calculation/production notes sit under native closed disclosures. Photo attribution and licence links are retained. Editing changes `updatedAt`/`editedAt`, not the original source-check or evidence-release dates.

Database editions still require the live publication and evidence gates. Any native Korean record, including a draft or withdrawal, blocks automatic translation fallback. A later English revision is not presented as Korean using a stale translation. No new database publication, collection execution or approval was performed during this change.

The operations screen separates collection success from site publication, last attempt from last success, and unavailable information from zero. City cards, state/city/search filters and reset controls expose failed, overdue, pending, blocked and unrun items. Scheduled deadlines are used where known; transaction-job delay is not guessed. Potentially mutating maintenance and publication controls remain manual and collapsed.

The portfolio test covers every English/Korean market brief and data story. Database tests exercise native-version precedence, withdrawal, missing translations and future English changes. Existing arithmetic tests recalculate the acquisition-duty and mortgage examples.

Browser tests exercise source disclosures, revised article navigation, and local-fixture admin filters without collection or publication writes. Production and preview deployment status must be checked separately from those tests; a protected preview must not have its authentication weakened for inspection.
